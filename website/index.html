Say the same thing, kindly.

Politely rewrites a blunt English sentence into a polite one that keeps its meaning. It is an LSTM encoder–decoder with attention and a copy mechanism, built and trained from scratch for an NLP course. The model below runs entirely in your browser.

How it works

Everything is written with PyTorch from basic layers: no pretrained model and no API. A sentence goes through five steps.

  1. Clean and split

    Lowercase, normalise quotes, and split into words and punctuation. Close the window now. becomes close · the · window · now · . and then ids 390 4 573 58 5.

  2. Read the sentence

    Each id becomes a 128-number embedding. An LSTM encoder (hidden size 256) reads the words one by one and keeps one state per word.

  3. Look back

    At every output step, Bahdanau attention scores all the input words and builds a weighted summary of the ones that matter now.

  4. Write or copy

    An LSTM decoder proposes the next word from a vocabulary of 1,805 words. A copy switch can instead take a word from your sentence, which keeps names, objects and numbers intact.

  5. Choose the sentence

    Greedy decoding takes the best word each time. Beam search keeps the five best partial sentences and returns the best complete one. It stops at the end-of-sentence token.

Training

28,800 sentence pairs, token-level cross-entropy, Adam (learning rate 0.001, halved on plateau), batch 64, dropout 0.3, teacher forcing, and early stopping on the validation loss. The final model trained for about 30 minutes on a CPU.

Attention makes learning much faster: the best validation loss comes at epoch 5 for B, against epoch 24 for A. Without copying, the training loss ends a little below the validation loss (slight overfitting, stopped by early stopping); with copying, both stay equal.

Results

An output counts as a success when a politeness classifier judges it polite, every content word of the input is kept, and no word is repeated. Three test sets go from easy to hard.

Copying made the biggest difference. On hand-written sentences, adding the copy mechanism raises success from 22% to 76%. A larger model, GRU cells or a different teacher forcing ratio did not help.

What works and what does not

Real outputs of the final model on sentences written by hand.

The model picks its polite frame from a few cue words. A refusal it does not recognise can turn into a request, and copied words keep their form even when the grammar needs a change.

The data

Public corpora for this task are full of insults, so the dataset was built for the project: a program combines situations, objects and people with 108 impolite and 54 polite phrasings written by hand. Every impolite sentence has polite versions with exactly the same content.
