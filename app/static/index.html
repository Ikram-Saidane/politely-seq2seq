    How it works

    Politely is a sequence-to-sequence network: an encoder reads the impolite sentence, a decoder writes the polite one word by word, an attention mechanism tells the decoder where to look, and a copy switch lets it reuse the words of the input. Everything was implemented and trained from scratch in PyTorch.

    1. PreprocessUnicode normalisation, lowercasing, then words and punctuation marks become separate tokens.close · the · window · now · .
    2. Vocabulary1,805 entries learned on the training split: words seen at least twice plus <PAD> <SOS> <EOS> <UNK>.390 · 4 · 573 · 58 · 5 · 2
    3. RepresentEach id becomes a 128-dimensional embedding vector, learned during training and shared by both sides.[B, n] → [B, n, 128]
    4. EncodeAn LSTM (hidden size 256) reads the sentence and produces one state per word; its last state starts the decoder.[B, n, 256]
    5. AttendAt every step the decoder scores each input state, turns the scores into weights with a softmax and builds a context vector.α = softmax(score)
    6. DecodeAn LSTM writes the polite sentence one word at a time, from <SOS> until it produces <EOS> (40 words at most).would · you · mind · closing …
    7. CopyA switch pgen mixes "generate a vocabulary word" with "copy an input word", so names, numbers and unknown words survive.P = p·Pvocab + (1−p)·α

    "Would you mind … ?" is the polite frame the decoder generates; "the window" is copied from the input; the impatient "now" becomes "as soon as possible".

    Inside one decoding step

    1. Embed the previous word (the true one in training, the model's own guess when generating).
    2. Score every encoder state hj against the decoder state s: scorej = vᵀ tanh(Wehj + Wds); padding is masked.
    3. Softmax the scores into attention weights αj (they sum to 1: the heat map of the Rewrite tab).
    4. Context vector c = Σ αj hj, fed to the LSTM with the word embedding.
    5. Output layer → probabilities over the 1,805 words; the copy switch adds the attention weights onto the input words.

    How it was trained

    Data
    28,800 synthetic pairs, 9 balanced categories, no insults
    Objective
    token-level cross-entropy, <PAD> ignored
    Optimiser
    Adam, learning rate 0.001, halved on plateau
    Batches
    64 sentences, dropout 0.3, gradient clipping 1.0
    Teacher forcing
    the decoder sees the true previous word (ratio 1.0)
    Stopping
    early stopping after 3 epochs without progress on validation
    Check
    every model first memorised 40 pairs (sanity test)
    Hardware
    CPU only, about 90 seconds per epoch

    Three ways to choose the next word

    Greedy

    Always take the most probable word. Fast and deterministic.

    Beam search

    Keep the k best partial sentences and compare whole sentences (score normalised by length). Used by default: best on the validation set.

    Sampling

    Draw the next word at random from the distribution, sharpened by a temperature. More varied phrasings, more risk of mistakes.
